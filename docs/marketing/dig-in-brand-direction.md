# Dig In — Brand & Product Direction
### Built entirely from the words you used

You didn't describe an app. You described a feeling, precisely, in about nine sentences. That's rarer than you think — most founders can only describe features. Everything below is your own language turned into decisions.

---

## THE PALETTE

| Name | Hex | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Matcha** | `#6E8259` | Primary. Buttons, active states, the logo. The grounding color. |
| **Frog** | `#96B36B` | Accent, small doses only. Success states, the moment something is saved. Never a background. |
| **Honey** | `#E0A32E` | The one that means *now*. Reserved for The Find and time-sensitive moments. Scarcity makes it work. Always a fill with bark text on top — never a thin line, never small text. |
| **Cream** | `#F3EADA` | Page background. Never pure white — white is clinical and you are not. |
| **Cashmere** | `#D8CAB4` | Cards, dividers, resting surfaces. The texture color. |
| **Bark** | `#33302A` | Text. A warm near-black, never `#000`. |

**The rule that keeps it from going muddy:** matcha and cream do 80% of the work. Honey appears on maybe one element per screen. If two honey things are on screen at once, one of them is wrong.

**The honey rule (this one is load-bearing):** yellow is the hardest accent color in interface design because it disappears against cream — yellow text on `#F3EADA` is effectively invisible, and a thin yellow line reads as a smudge. So honey is *always* a filled shape with bark text sitting on it. Never a border, never a label, never an icon on its own. Obey that and yellow is the best choice available to you. Ignore it and the app looks broken on the first screen.

**Seasonality:** you love green nature and flowers, so let the app know what month it is. The accent shifts subtly through the year — deeper green in spring, more sunset in autumn. Small, quiet, and it makes people feel the app is alive.

## TYPOGRAPHY

**Serif for the feeling. Sans for the plan.**

That split is your whole brand in a typographic system. The emotional layer — greetings, the daily line, essay text, the question the app asks you — is set in a soft serif, because serif reads like a book and you told me the feeling you want is *turning a page in a good book*. The functional layer — lists, buttons, times, prices, quantities — is clean sans, because a grocery list should not be romantic.

**Free pairings that work:** Fraunces or Instrument Serif for display, Inter for interface. Both free, both excellent.

**Never bold anything mid-sentence.** Your voice is soft. Bold is shouting.

---

## TEXTURE — THE CASHMERE RULE

You said cashmere and I think that's the most useful word in your whole brief, because it's a *tactile* instruction for a thing nobody can touch.

What it means in practice: no hard edges, no pure white, no glossy surfaces, no drop shadows pretending to be depth. Generous padding — cashmere is not tight. Matte fills. Rounded but not bubbly. A faint paper grain on large surfaces, so cream reads as *stock* rather than *screen*.

**The test:** does this screen look like it would be warm if you touched it? If it looks cold, it's wrong.

## THE FEELINGS, TURNED INTO FEATURES

This is the part I'd protect. These aren't decoration — each one is a real product decision derived from something you said, and together they're the reason Dig In wouldn't feel like anything else.

### 1. The page turn
> *"like turning a page while reading a good book"*

Make it literal. Every transition between screens is a page turn — not a slide, not a fade. Soft, weighted, with a little resistance. It's the single most ownable interaction decision available to you, it costs almost nothing to build, and nobody in this category has taken it.

Your plans are chapters. Your week is a spread. The metaphor holds all the way down.

### 2. The Find
> *"the feeling when you find a great deal while shopping and you have to buy it in that moment"*

One thing a day. Surfaced in honey. Time-boxed — it expires. A restaurant with a table tonight in your budget, a market that has the thing you can never find, a deal on something you actually need.

That feeling you described is the app's dopamine, and it's the reason someone opens it when they don't need to. **This is your retention mechanic and you already knew it — you just described it as a feeling instead of a feature.**

### 3. The groove
> *"what meshes with your groove of life"*

Not a profile. Not a settings page. A *groove* — the app learns your rhythm and stops asking. Sunday is nine people. Tuesday is alone and tired. You don't eat before noon. It should know that within three weeks and never ask again.

This is your control pillar as software: fewer questions over time, not more.

### 4. Getting ready with a friend
> *"the feeling of being on FaceTime with a friend while you're getting ready to go out"*

That's the **voice**. Second person, present tense, warm, a little bit hurried in a good way. "Okay — you've got forty minutes and you said you wanted something green." Never instructional. Never a coach. A friend who's already dressed and waiting.

And it's a feature too: let two people plan the same evening at the same time, seeing each other's picks. Nobody's built that well, and loneliness is your whole thesis.

### 5. Walk to it
> *"I love walking in different cities"*

The app should think in walks, not just meals. Distance and route as a first-class part of every suggestion — "twelve minutes, past the park." You've just made a food app also a reason to leave the house, which is the actual product.

## THE VOICE

Sentence case. Contractions. Short. Never exclamation marks. Never "simply" or "just" or "easy" — those words presume, and presuming is condescending.

**She says:** "You've got forty minutes."
**She never says:** "Let's get cooking! 🎉"

The app talks the way you do — quietly, and only when it has something worth saying.

---

## ON BOLDNESS

You said: *"my food, my body, my choice."*

That's a strong line and it does exactly what you want it to. One flag, and then it's yours: that phrasing carries an unmistakable echo of abortion-rights language, which for your audience may be precisely the point — you've said you want to be unapologetic. Just choose it knowingly rather than discover it in a comment section. Either decision is defensible; being surprised by it isn't.

**A softer sibling if you want the same spine with less charge:** *Bold about what I put in my body.*

---

## THE ONE LINE

Everything you said orbits this:

> **Dig In helps you find out what makes you happy — and then puts it on the calendar.**

That's the whole company. Discovery plus system. The two halves of you, in one sentence.

---

## THE PART THAT ISN'T ABOUT DESIGN

You wrote that those years were stolen, and that you blame yourself for being overwhelmed by things that pulled you off your goals.

I'd gently put down half of that. Being overwhelmed by things outside your control isn't a failure of discipline — it's what being overwhelmed *is*. You've built extraordinary systems for food, money, hosting, and travel. The one system that isn't serving you is the one that files "I was struggling" under "I failed."

The excitement in your message is the real signal. You're not describing an escape from your life. You're describing walking through a city in good light, finding something beautiful you weren't expecting, and having somewhere to put it.

That's not a vacation. That's the product.
