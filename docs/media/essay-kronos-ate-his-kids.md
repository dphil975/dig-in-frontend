# Kronos ate his kids

*In which I binge four thousand years of Greek family drama, realize it's about me, and pitch a prestige TV show nobody asked for.*

---

So I've been unemployed-adjacent and bored, which is a specific flavor of bored, and I have responded by watching the entire catalog of Greek mythology. Then Egyptian. I'm working through it like a syllabus. I have opinions now. I have *takes.*

And I need to tell you: those people were **messy.** Not messy like your cousin. Messy like everyone in the family is actively trying to kill each other while pretending it's about honor.

Here's the one that got me. Kronos — the father — is told his children will overthrow him. So he eats them. Not metaphorically. He eats his own kids as they're born, one by one, to keep his position. His wife finally hides the last one, Zeus, wraps a rock in a blanket and hands it over, and Kronos eats the rock because he's not paying attention, because men like that never are.

Zeus grows up in a cave. Comes back. Frees his siblings. Overthrows his father.

And then — this is the part — **becomes a completely different kind of problem.** Not the same problem. He doesn't eat anybody. He's a trickster, he's charming, he lies constantly, and his behavior wrecks his brothers and eventually his own children for generations.

I have been thinking about that for a week and a half.

---

## Give me the show, cowards

Real quick, because I need to get this out of my system: why are the adaptations so bad?

We keep getting movies about the fighting. Big CGI, sword, lightning, someone yells. And the fighting is *the least interesting part.* The story is the betrayal. It's a boy whose father showed him no mercy, who had to become the responsible sibling before he was ready, whose brothers trained in secret to get their independence back from a parent who was never going to give it. That's the show. Six episodes before anyone even gets a throne.

And then — season two — the actual tragedy: he wins, and he becomes someone whose tricks cause the exact damage he escaped. Not because he's evil. Because nobody taught him another way to hold power.

Less fighting. More "what did you think was going to happen." I would watch nine seasons. Somebody call me.

---

## Okay, so why am I actually obsessed with this

Because I got better, and getting better did something I wasn't warned about.

When your brain is loud, you cannot see your own life. The volume is the whole experience. Everything is a tidal wave and you're just trying to stay upright in it — my emotions used to come out as these enormous complicated swells that took the whole day with them.

I'm in the right treatment now. And it's calm. Genuinely calm, in a way I didn't know was available.

And here's what nobody tells you: **once it goes quiet, you can see everything.** Every problem in your life, clearly, in order, with a little label on it saying where it came from. It's like someone finally turned the lights on in a room you'd been navigating by feel for years, and you go, oh. Oh, there's a lot of furniture in here. Some of it isn't mine. Somebody else left it.

And then the second thing arrives, which is worse: at a certain age, you become responsible for what you do with all of it.

Not responsible for what happened. Let me be extremely clear about that, because people get this wrong and use it to hand the bill to the wrong person. You're not responsible for the damage that was done to you. You are responsible for what you carry it into. For the room. For the people in front of you now.

That's the whole Zeus problem. He didn't deserve what Kronos did. And he was still on the hook for what he became afterward.

That's a horrible, freeing thing to figure out on a Tuesday.

---

## Also I'm scared of ending up on a meme page

Small confession, related.

Part of what keeps me composed now is that I know if I lose it in public — if I have a moment that doesn't look the way people think a reasonable reaction looks — somebody's filming. And I get to be a fifteen-second clip with a caption for people to laugh at, permanently, forever.

That's a genuinely new pressure and I don't think we've reckoned with it. Every generation before this one got to have a bad day in public and have it just... end. Now a bad day is content. A hard moment is a *format.*

So we're all a little more managed than we used to be, and we call it maturity, and some of it is maturity, but some of it is surveillance and we should probably say that out loud.

---

## The difference between telling you something and performing it

Which brings me to the thing that's been bugging me all month.

When I tell someone I have OCD, I'm doing it as a *courtesy.* I'm saying: hey, there are things I do that will make no sense to you, here's the context, and it's not okay if I'm an ass about it. That's disclosure. It's functional. It's for you.

But scroll for ten minutes and you'll watch a stranger perform the most intimate moment of their year on Al Gore's internet — crying about a diagnosis, filming a breakup, narrating a family estrangement — and I'm sitting there like, sister, for what? So four hundred people you'll never meet can type "omg yes girl"?

And I don't say that to be cruel, because I actually think I know what's happening.

**Most people don't need the internet. They need the physical strength of a community they've been deprived of.** They need somebody in the room. They need a person who'll sit on the floor with them, or bring food, or just be *there* while it's hard. They can't get that, so they're taking the closest available substitute, which is strangers, which does not work, which is why they have to keep doing it.

That's not vanity. That's a shortage.

---

## Why I don't finish things

Okay, the hard one.

I start projects and I don't finish them, and for a long time I thought that was discipline. It's not. I finally figured out what it is.

**I don't finish things because the environment I'm in can't taste the win.**

You finish something, and you look up, and the people around you don't know what they're looking at. Not out of malice — they just don't have the context to know that the thing you made is hard, or good, or a real leap from the last one. So the gratitude never lands. The accomplishment doesn't convert. And your brain, which is a very simple animal, notes that finishing produced nothing and quietly stops funding the project.

Here's what I've decided, and it's a generalization but I'll defend it: **the people who understand your vision are almost never in the room you're currently standing in.** That's not a tragedy and it's not about anyone being bad. It's just a mismatch of context.

Which means the fix isn't more discipline. It's not a better morning routine. It's *finding the room.* Go where one person can look at the thing and immediately understand why it's difficult. One is enough. One person who gets it will restart an engine that a year of self-improvement content won't touch.

I spent a long time trying to fix my follow-through when the problem was my address.

## The nap situation

I nap when I'm bored. I nap when I'm irritated. I nap when I don't have the answer I'm looking for.

It's called tummy time. Face down, floor, carpet only, Darrell the octopus, dog brings Lammy. It works, and I stand by it.

But I'll be honest — I don't want that to be the entire architecture of my life. There's a difference between rest and a trapdoor, and I've been using it as a trapdoor. Bored, drop. Irritated, drop. Stuck, drop.

(Related: I also fall asleep to videos where the person has a boring voice, and I've decided my brain is absorbing it anyway, like Oddish's attack, and I'll wake up with new skills. Sleeper agent method. I've had no evidence for this in four years and I'm not letting it go.)

---

## I love routine and I'm bored out of my mind

Both things. At once. This is the actual condition.

I love routine. I love organization. I love knowing what's happening. And I am *so bored* doing the same thing over and over, and what I actually want — which I know is insane — is **the same input to produce a different outcome every day.**

I don't want to be a spectator in my own life. I love playing The Sims. I refuse to *be* played as one. Watching a little person do the same eight actions in the same four rooms while somebody else decides what she wants — no thank you. Not this year.

So the answer isn't less routine, because routine is the only reason I function. The answer is putting something unpredictable *inside* the routine. Same structure, different content. Same Sunday, new people at the table. Same walk, different street.

That's the whole design problem of my life and I think I finally named it.

---

## My skills are skills, actually

Something I've been undervaluing, and I want to say it plainly because I think some of you are doing the same thing.

I can find a deal on anything. Not "I'm a good shopper" — I mean I can find the thing, then find who actually *manufactures* the thing, and go straight to the source. I can look at a product I like, read the ingredients, cross-reference them, and make a version at home that's better and costs a quarter. When something becomes my safe item I will stock it deep because I refuse to be without it. I make my own coffee because I want total control over it.

For years I filed all of that under "quirks."

That's sourcing. That's product development. That's supply chain. People get paid extremely well for exactly those three skills and I've been doing them for free since I was a teenager because they came out of anxiety instead of a business degree.

(And yes I still buy coffee sometimes, as a treat. But randomly. Because if you treat yourself constantly then the treat stops being a reward and becomes a baseline, and then when something big happens you can't feel it. Anyway rewards are for dogs. I'm a princess.)

---

## The flowers came up

I planted pink flowers a while back and they finally bloomed and they are *so* vibrant against the sun right now. The clouds are enormous and fluffy. The breeze is out there trying its absolute hardest to cool this off and failing completely, and I respect the effort. Same, girl. Same.

I walked the dog. I made pancakes. I worked on my coding project. I took tummy time. (I'm still salty about the sewing.)

And I keep waking up with a "what are we even doing here" attitude some mornings, which is a strange thing to sit next to how grateful I am. Because — and here's the part I don't say much — **I never thought I'd live this long.** So the fact that I'm out here with a garden, and a routine, and a dog with a Lammy, and a project I care about, is genuinely absurd to me. In a good way. Mostly.

The catch-up feeling is the hard part. Watching people hit milestones on a schedule while I'm only *now* in a position to take care of myself. That's a real grief and I'm not going to pretend it isn't. Sometimes you don't want a plan. You want somebody to hold you and tell you you're doing your best.

But nobody's coming with that, and I'm not going to be a spectator, so.

I want to go to Greece. I want to stand in Italy and learn about the Roman Empire from someone who's obsessed with it. I want to hear what these people actually thought about each other four thousand years ago, because the pettiness is *identical* and that's the most comforting thing I've learned all year.

And I want to make something I'm proud to admit I made. Not viral. Not monetized. Made.

Kronos ate his kids. Zeus got out of the cave and became a different kind of problem.

I'd like to be the third thing.

*— see you next Sunday. Tell me the skill you've been filing under "quirk." I bet it's a job.*
