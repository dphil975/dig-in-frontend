# "The Blow Pops are smaller now" — Notes & Reels

Companion assets for the essay. One piece of writing, two weeks of posting.

---

## PART 1 — SUBSTACK NOTES

Text-first, no hashtags, no links except the essay one. Post 1–2 a day. The goal is not to promote the essay — it's to be a person in the feed who says things. The essay link goes out once, on Sunday, and never again.

**1 — the cold open**
> The Blow Pops are smaller. I'm not being dramatic, I did the research, by which I mean I bought one and had a full crisis in a parking lot.

**2 — the thesis, standalone**
> Everything got smaller. The wrapper stayed the same size.
>
> That's healthcare, that's rent, that's content, that's dating. Big packaging, no gum.

**3 — the healthcare one (this will get the most replies)**
> Comparison shopping for medication should not be a life skill. Prices should simply be prices. Instead I'm out here playing Tetris for the privilege of continuing to be a functional person.

**4 — the wait times**
> "The next available appointment is in four months."
>
> Sister. I could grow an entire new personality in four months.

**5 — the one that will get quoted back to you**
> I don't mind that people have help. I mind the performance.
>
> Taking the ladder and then describing the climb — that's the part that hurts people. Not the money. The lie about the money.

**6 — content decline**
> Content didn't get worse because people got dumber. It got worse because the reason changed.
>
> People used to post because they loved something. Now they post because they're lonely and broke and someone told them this was the exit. You can feel the difference.

**7 — the regional thing (genuinely original observation, let it breathe)**
> Scroll for twenty minutes and notice that everyone in one metro area is making the same video. Same framing, same audio, same three beats.
>
> That's not a culture. That's one person doing well and forty people reverse-engineering it by Tuesday.

**8 — editing as respect**
> Editing is a form of respect. When something's tight, that's not showing off — that's somebody saying "I thought about you before I hit post."
>
> Sloppy tells me you thought about you.

**9 — the fake abundance**
> We have more access to media than any humans in history and it is so hard to find anything. You scroll for forty minutes and come away with nothing.
>
> The abundance is fake. Big wrapper, no gum.

**10 — dating**
> I confirm a plan the day before, the morning of, and one hour prior, and I will not apologize for it.
>
> You didn't get sick. You got scared. Those are different and I've already washed my hair.

**11 — tummy time (the warm one, post this after a spiky one)**
> Pity naps in this house are called tummy time. Face down, on the floor, must be carpet, non-negotiable. Darrell the octopus. The dog brings Lammy.
>
> Works better than most things people charge money for.

**12 — the close**
> Joy isn't something you find right now. It's something you manufacture.
>
> Nobody's handing it to you and the world is not going to calm down in time. Make your own gum.

**13 — the ask (post Thursday, after the essay has settled)**
> What got smaller in your life this month? Same wrapper, less inside.
>
> I'm collecting them. Genuinely.

## PART 2 — REELS

### REEL 1 — "The Blow Pop"
*Cold open, prop-driven, funniest. This is your reach piece.*

**Hook (0–3s)** — extreme close-up, a Blow Pop held between two fingers, rotating slowly. No talking.
> On-screen: *they shrunk it*

**VO (soft, aggrieved, completely serious):**
> They shrunk the Blow Pop.
>
> I'm not being dramatic. I did the research. By which I mean I bought one, held it in my hand, and had a full crisis in a parking lot.
>
> The bubblegum center — the entire reason for its existence, the payoff, the *treat* — is now a rumor. You have to work through the whole lollipop for a suggestion of gum.
>
> That's a trick. I didn't sign up for a trick. I signed up for a treat.
>
> And I know. Of all the things happening right now, I've chosen candy.
>
> But I've been sitting with it and I think the Blow Pop is the whole thing.
>
> Everything got smaller. The wrapper stayed the same size.

**Visual beats:**
- 0–3s: the lollipop, rotating, sunlight
- 3–10s: you in a parked car, deadpan, holding it up like evidence
- 10–18s: hard cuts — a receipt, a pill bottle, a rent listing, a phone scrolling fast
- 18–24s: back to your face, still holding the Blow Pop, still deadpan
- Final frame: the lollipop set down on a table. Silence.

**On-screen text:** `they shrunk it` → `I had a crisis in a parking lot` → `it's not about candy` → `everything got smaller` → `the wrapper stayed the same`

**Caption:**
> They shrunk the Blow Pop and I've been unwell about it for three weeks.
>
> But here's the thing — once you notice it you notice it everywhere. Healthcare. Rent. Content. Dating. Everything got smaller and the packaging stayed identical, and you're supposed to pretend you didn't clock it.
>
> Full essay is up. What got smaller in your life this month? 👇

### REEL 2 — "Four Months"
*The anger reel. Highest comment volume by far — everyone has this story.*

**Hook (0–3s)** — you, phone to your ear, on hold. The hold music is audible. You say nothing for three full seconds.
> On-screen: *minute 41*

**VO:**
> You call around. You compare prices — which should not be a thing, prices should simply *be* prices, but here we are, comparison shopping for the privilege of continuing to be a functional person.
>
> You find someone affordable. Not taking new patients.
>
> You find someone taking new patients. Doesn't take your insurance.
>
> You find someone who takes your insurance.
>
> Next appointment: four months.
>
> Sister. I could grow an entire new personality in four months.
>
> And even *with* insurance you're waiting on a stranger in an office to approve something your doctor already said you need. Two people who went to school for this agreed — and a third party who has never met you gets to say "let me think about it" for eleven business days.
>
> Being an adult is supposed to mean getting the help you need. But the responsible thing and the affordable thing are almost never the same thing, and then everyone acts surprised when people stop going.

**Visual beats:** hold music throughout, low. You on the floor with your back against a cabinet, phone on speaker beside you. Never stand up. The whole reel is one static shot of a person slowly losing altitude.

**On-screen text:** `not taking new patients` → `doesn't take your insurance` → `four months` → `I could grow a whole new personality`

**Caption:**
> Playing Tetris with my own medication is not a life skill I agreed to develop.
>
> The wait times have me in orbit. Genuinely orbital, I have left the atmosphere.
>
> Tell me your number. How long is your next available? 👇

**Note:** reply to every single comment on this one. This thread is a community, not a comment section — and it's the clearest proof you'll ever get that the loneliness problem and the logistics problem are the same problem.

### REEL 3 — "I Don't Mind That You Have Help"
*The sharpest one. Most likely to be screenshotted and reposted. Post it third, once the first two have warmed people up.*

**Hook (0–3s)** — you, straight to camera, no preamble, very calm.
> On-screen: *let me be precise about this*

**VO:**
> I got ghosted on an apartment. Normal. Adulthood is sixty percent being ghosted by people who have something you need.
>
> Two weeks later it shows up on my timeline. *My* apartment. Somebody else doing a walkthrough with a caption about manifestation.
>
> And I want to be precise, because I've been turning this over all month.
>
> I do not care that people have help. I really don't. Somebody's parents co-signed. Somebody's partner covers rent. That's the lottery of who you were born to and being mad at individuals for it is exhausting and pointless.
>
> What I mind is the performance.
>
> The part where the help gets edited out and what's left is a story about vision boards and waking up at 5am. When you take the ladder and then describe the climb.
>
> Because that's the part that actually hurts people. Not the money. The lie about the money.
>
> There's a whole generation out here thinking they're failing at a game other people are playing on a different difficulty setting. And nobody will say it out loud.
>
> Anyway. I deleted the app. I'm not proud. I lay on the floor.

**Visual beats:** one unbroken take, soft window light, no cuts. Let the stillness do it. The only edit is a hard cut to black on the last line.

**On-screen text:** `I don't mind that you have help` → `I mind the performance` → `you took the ladder` → `then described the climb`

**Caption:**
> I don't mind that people have help. I mind the performance.
>
> Not the money — the lie about the money. A whole generation thinks it's failing at a game other people are playing on a different difficulty setting, and nobody will say it out loud.
>
> Saying it out loud.

**Note:** don't add music. Don't add a trending sound. This one works because it's quiet and specific, and any production on top of it makes it feel like a hot take instead of a thought.
