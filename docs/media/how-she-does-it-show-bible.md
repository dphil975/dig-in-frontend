# HOW SHE DOES IT
## Show Bible

## THE SHOW

**Title:** How She Does It
*(Runner-up: **Nobody Taught Us** — more literary, ties directly to your Substack thesis. "How She Does It" wins because it's a question women actually ask each other, and because it points straight at the app. Check handle and podcast-name availability before you commit to either.)*

**Logline:**
> Women at every stage of adulthood, talking honestly about what nobody warned them about — and the system they built to survive it.

**The premise in one line:** every woman you know has figured something out. Nobody has ever asked her how.

**The three rules that make it different:**

**1. It ends with a system, every time.** Not a lesson. Not a takeaway. An actual repeatable thing she does — the Sunday reset, the envelope method, the two-alarm rule. This is the difference between a podcast and a resource, and it's the reason the show can feed a product.

**2. It's not curated.** You said it exactly right: we never get clear voices, everything is polished into uselessness. So the format is built to break polish — specific Tuesdays instead of general themes, silence left in, no pre-scripted questions sent ahead.

**3. It is never negative.** No takedowns, no drama, no reaction content, ever. This is a stated editorial position, published on the site. It's also strategically brilliant: the snark economy has nothing to grab onto in a show that never attacks anyone. You don't need to fight it. You just don't give it a handle.

## THE STRUCTURE

**Five stages. Every season climbs the whole ladder.**

| Stage | Age | What this episode is about |
|---|---|---|
| **The beginning** | 18–25 | First apartment, first real money, learning to feed yourself. Nobody has clear voices from here — it's all either mocked or marketed to. |
| **The scramble** | 26–35 | Career, partnership, the catch-up feeling, the first time you realize the plan isn't happening on schedule. |
| **The reckoning** | 36–49 | What you thought would happen versus what did. The quiet renegotiation. |
| **The rewrite** | 50–65 | Second acts. Divorce, reinvention, the house getting quiet, going back to something you left. |
| **The record** | 65+ | The women nobody asked. Your anchor episodes — funniest, most surprising, most likely to make someone cry in a car. |

**Why the ladder matters:** listeners get to hear forward and backward at once. A 27-year-old hears a 61-year-old say *"I did the exact thing you're doing and here's what it cost me"* — and a 61-year-old hears a 22-year-old and remembers who she was. That vertical is the whole product. Almost every women's show is horizontal — one age, talking to itself.

## EPISODE ANATOMY

**Length:** 35–45 minutes. Video and audio. Cook while you talk, exactly like the hostel format — side by side, hands busy, eye contact optional.

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The stage** | Where she is right now. Not a resume — a Tuesday. What time she got up, what she ate, what's on the counter. |
| **What nobody warned you about** | The opening ritual question. Same every episode. |
| **The hurt** | The part of this stage that women don't say out loud. Let it be uncomfortable. Don't rescue her. |
| **The unreasonable thing** | Recurring segment. The irrational thing she does that *works*. No shame, no explaining it away. |
| **The system** | What she built. Specific, named, repeatable. This is the payoff. |
| **The handoff** | "What would you tell a woman ten years behind you?" She's talking to a real person, not the audience. |

## THE TWO RITUAL QUESTIONS

Every episode opens and closes the same way. Consistency is what turns a podcast into a format people can quote.

**Opening:**
> **"What did nobody warn you about?"**

**Closing:**
> **"So what's the system?"**

The hurt at the top, the fix at the bottom. That's the arc of every episode and it's also the arc of your entire brand — you don't leave people in the feeling, you hand them a structure and let them out.

## THE UNREASONABLE THING

This is the segment that will make the show spread, so protect it.

Every woman does something completely irrational that keeps her functional. She keeps a spare of everything because running out sends her into a spiral. She won't go to bed with dishes in the sink even at 2am. She confirms plans three times. She eats the same lunch for eleven months at a stretch. She takes naps on the floor.

Nobody says these out loud because we've been trained to think they're symptoms. **They're not symptoms. They're load-bearing.**

You ask, she answers, nobody laughs at her, and then you ask the follow-up that makes it a real segment:

> **"What does it protect you from?"**

That's the show. That's the whole thesis about womanhood having levels nobody talks about, delivered through a lollipop-sized question.

It's also your single best clip format — 45 seconds, funny, warm, deeply relatable, and it never mocks the person. Every woman watching sees her own unreasonable thing and feels less alone for eleven seconds. That's the business.

## HOW TO INTERVIEW SO IT ISN'T CURATED

You named the problem precisely: everything is curated and you never actually hear the person. Here's how you break that.

**Never send questions ahead.** A prepared answer is a press release. Tell them the shape of the conversation — stages, systems, no gotchas — and nothing else. Say explicitly: *there's no wrong answer and you can stop anytime.*

**Ask about a specific day, not a theme.** "What's it like being a single mom" gets you a speech. "Walk me through last Tuesday from 6am" gets you a life. Always go for the Tuesday.

**Ask for the number, the brand, the exact thing.** Specificity is the antidote to polish. Not "I budget carefully" — *what app, what day, what happens when it doesn't work.*

**Let silence run for five seconds.** Your instinct will be to rescue people. Don't. The sentence after the pause is the real one.

**Never fix her mid-answer.** No advice, no "have you tried." You're not a coach. You asked because you actually want to know.

**Cook.** Hands busy, side by side. Same reason as the hostel show — women have had every important conversation of their lives standing next to another woman at a counter.

**The care rule:** you said women want to hear stories from someone who actually cares about the woman she's talking to. That's audible. Listeners can hear the difference between a host running a segment and a host who's genuinely interested, and it is not fakeable. So only book people you're actually curious about. If you're not curious, don't record it.

## THE APP BRIDGE

You asked how this flows into Dig In. It doesn't flow — it *is* the app, in audio form. Here's the mechanism.

**Every episode produces one named system.** "Marisol's Sunday reset." "Deb's envelope method." "Priya's two-alarm rule." Named after the woman who built it, credited permanently.

**Those systems live in the app.** A browsable section — real systems from real women, attributed, usable. That's not a marketing tie-in, that's genuine product content that would otherwise cost you a fortune to invent.

**And it solves your hardest product problem.** Your app is supposed to learn someone's groove. Right now the grooves would be invented by you. This way they're documented from real women at real stages, and a 24-year-old can literally load a 58-year-old's system and try it on.

**Why guests will promote it:** they get a permanent page with their name on a system they built. Not a plug, not an ad read — a credit. People share the thing that has their name on it, forever, without being asked.

**The full loop:**

> episode → named system → lives in the app → app users hear where it came from → they listen → they submit their own unreasonable thing → that becomes an episode

**And the money flows the way you wanted it to:** affiliate links on whatever the system requires · the paid Substack tier holding the full templates · ticketed dinners where guests and listeners sit at the same table · brand partners who fit the system, not the demographic. Nothing here requires you to be negative about anyone. The revenue comes from being *useful*, which is the only version of this you'd actually enjoy doing.

## ON HAPPINESS, BECAUSE YOU SAID THE TRUEST THING

> *"I think a lot of people don't realize what true happiness feels like because they're so used to being disappointed all the time."*

Make that a recurring question. Near the end, before the handoff:

> **"When's the last time something went the way you hoped?"**

Watch what happens. Most people will have to think, and the thinking is the answer. Some will say they can't remember. A few will name something so small it breaks your heart — a bus that came on time, a plant that lived.

That question is the emotional center of your entire show, and it's the thing that separates it from every other women's podcast: you're not asking about trauma and you're not asking about success. You're asking whether hope has been paying out.

## SEASON ONE

**Ten episodes, one full climb through the stages, then repeat.**

Order: 65+ · 26–35 · 18–25 · 50–65 · 36–49 · 65+ · 26–35 · 50–65 · 18–25 · 36–49

**Start with an elder.** Two reasons: she'll be the most generous with a first-time host and forgive your mistakes, and she sets the tone of the show as serious and warm before anyone can mistake it for a lifestyle podcast.

**Book people you already know for the first four.** Your mom's friends. Your neighbor. The woman at the co-op. You need the reps somewhere safe, and the truth is your first three interviews will be worse than your fourth no matter who's in the chair.

**Safety and consent:** everything in the protection plan still applies — LLC, business address, releases before recording, guest review pass before publish, published ethics policy, no naming living third parties. Going 18+ across the board solves the consent problem entirely, which is exactly why this version of the show is the right one.
