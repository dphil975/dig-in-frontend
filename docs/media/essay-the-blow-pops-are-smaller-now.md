# The Blow Pops are smaller now

*A month-end inventory, in which I delete an app over an apartment, take a pity nap on the floor, and try to schedule joy like it's a dentist appointment.*

---

Okay so. The Blow Pops are smaller.

I need you to understand that I am not being dramatic, I did the research, by which I mean I bought one, held it in my hand, and had a full crisis in a parking lot. They shrunk it. The bubblegum center — the entire reason for the Blow Pop's existence, the payoff, the *treat* — has been reduced to a rumor. You have to work through the whole lollipop for what is now essentially a suggestion of gum. That's a trick. I did not sign up for a trick. I signed up for a treat.

And listen, I know. I *know.* Of all the things happening in the world right now, I have chosen to be personally aggrieved about candy. But I've been sitting with it all month and I think the Blow Pop is actually the whole thing. Because once you notice it, you notice it everywhere.

Everything got smaller. The wrapper stayed the same size.

---

## Exhibit A: trying to see a doctor in this country

Let me tell you about playing Tetris with my own medication.

You call around. You compare prices, which — side note — should not be a thing you have to do, prices should simply *be prices*, but here we are, and I'm out here comparison shopping for the privilege of continuing to be a functioning person. You find someone affordable. They're not taking new patients. You find someone taking new patients. They don't take your insurance. You find someone who takes your insurance. The next appointment is in four months.

Four months! Sister, I could grow a whole new personality in four months.

And even *with* insurance you're sitting there waiting for some stranger in an office to approve a thing your doctor already said you need. Which is such a specific kind of insane. Two people who went to school for this agreed, and then a third party who has never met you gets to say "hmm, let me think about it" for eleven business days.

The wait times have me going into orbit. Genuinely orbital. I have left the atmosphere.

Here's the part that actually gets me though. Being an adult is supposed to mean getting the help you need so you can become the best version of yourself — or, let's be honest, so you can avoid a much worse decade later. That's the deal. That's what "taking care of yourself" is supposed to mean. But the system is built so that the responsible thing and the affordable thing are almost never the same thing, and then everybody acts surprised when people stop going.

I survived a stroke. And I still find myself doing the math on whether an appointment is worth it. That should tell you everything about how this is set up.

## Exhibit B: the apartment, and why I deleted the app

So I got ghosted. Normal. Fine. Adulthood is 60% being ghosted by people who have something you need.

Then two weeks later the apartment shows up on my timeline. *My* apartment. The one I toured. Somebody else is standing in it doing a walkthrough video with a caption about manifestation.

Fewer than five thousand followers, by the way. Not that it matters, except that it *does* matter, because the energy was very "as you all know" and sister, we do not all know. We do not know you.

And I want to be so clear about this, because I've been turning it over all month and I think the distinction matters: **I do not care that people have help.** I really don't. Somebody's parents co-signed. Somebody's partner covers rent. Somebody got a check when their grandmother died. That's life, that's luck, that's the lottery of who you were born to, and being mad at individual people for it is exhausting and also pointless.

What I mind is the *performance.* The part where the help gets edited out and what's left is a story about vision boards and getting up at 5am. When you take the ladder and then describe the climb.

Because that's the thing that actually hurts people. Not the money — the lie about the money. There's a whole generation out here thinking they're failing at a game other people are playing on a different difficulty setting, and nobody will say it out loud.

Anyway I deleted the app. I'm not proud. I licked my wounds. I lay on the floor. (More on the floor situation later.)

## Exhibit C: everyone became an influencer because everyone is lonely

Here's my actual theory, and I've been building it all month.

Content got worse. Across the board. Not because people got dumber, but because the *reason* for making it changed.

People used to post because they loved something. A craft, a bit, a fandom, a stupid recurring joke. Now people post because they're bored and lonely and broke and someone told them this was the exit. And you can feel it. You can feel the difference between somebody who made a thing and somebody who *needed* the thing to work.

Watch how it clusters by region. Pay attention some time — scroll for twenty minutes and notice that everyone in a given metro area is making the same video. Same framing, same audio, same three beats. That's not a coincidence and it's not a culture. That's one person doing well and forty people reverse-engineering it by Tuesday.

And then there's the ten-second video that's a bar-for-bar copy of another ten-second video, and it's not funny, and it wasn't funny the first time, and it sends me into *orbit.*

Also — and I say this with love — I don't want the dissertation. I don't want eleven minutes of someone speaking very slowly about a mundane thing with a jazz loop under it. Get to the point. Editing is a form of respect. When something is tight, that's not showing off, that's somebody saying *I thought about you before I hit post.* Sloppy tells me you thought about you.

Not everyone is good at everything and that is genuinely fine — I am bad at sewing, we'll get there. But we've built a system where people with no craft get launched to the top, and then everyone's confused when there's nothing underneath. They're mad the person can't deliver, when the person never claimed to. We picked them. We wanted somebody to idolize and we grabbed the nearest available body.

If you want proof that this is about culture and not talent, watch Love Island US and then watch Love Island UK back to back. Same format. Same premise. Completely different species. The editing, the pacing, the way people talk to each other, what's considered rude, what's considered funny. It'll have you sitting back on the couch going *what exactly is wrong with us.* (Jasmine, though. Jasmine is 2005 hot girl and I am obsessed. That's not analysis, that's just true.)

## Exhibit D: you're not even allowed to have a hobby anymore

Try to read something online — paywall. Try to get the book instead — someone's trying to ban it. Try to join a fan space and enjoy a thing purely and stupidly — get shamed for liking it wrong.

We have more access to media than any humans in history and it is *so hard* to find anything. You scroll for forty minutes and come away with nothing. The abundance is fake. It's the Blow Pop again — big wrapper, no gum.

And dating. Oh, dating.

I plan. I love to plan. I will confirm a plan the day before, the morning of, and one hour prior, and I will not apologize for it. Why? Because I'm not spending gas and energy and an outfit on somebody who's going to text "sorry, not feeling great" ninety minutes out. Sister, do not piss me off. You didn't get sick, you got scared, and those are different, and I've already washed my hair.

Flakiness got rebranded as boundaries and I need us to unrebrand it.

## What I actually did this month, and why it counts

Here's where I have to be fair, because I've been complaining beautifully for a thousand words and today was, actually, a great day.

I had toast with cinnamon sugar butter. (Don't. I'm a growing girl.) I walked to Dutch Bros, and by walked I mean from the car to the window, which is still technically ambulation. I got a protein sugar-free thing, because sugar-free is a get-out-of-jail-free card and that's just math. It's PEMDAS. And it's too hot to be outside, so if I sweat that's cardio, and clenching your abs in a parked car is a workout. I don't make the rules.

I did laundry without being guilted into it. I worked on my coding project. I tested recipes. I had leftovers, twice, like a woman with a *system.* I made iced coffee in a French press and drank it out of a teapot — one part espresso, one part caramel coffee, chocolate sauce, a lil half and half, oat milk to finish — and I want to be clear that the teapot was not a bit. The teapot was the only clean vessel and it turned out to be the best cup of coffee I've had this month.

I tried sewing. I lasted one hour. Then I took a pity nap, which in this house is called tummy time: face down, on the floor, must be carpet, non-negotiable, with Darrell the octopus Squishmallow. The dog brings Lammy. We both go down like we've been sedated. Ten out of ten, would recommend, it works better than most things people charge money for.

And that's the whole point I've been circling.

**Joy is not something you find right now. It's something you manufacture.** Nobody is going to hand it to you, the algorithm isn't going to serve it to you, and the world is not going to calm down in time. The wrapper is going to keep being the same size while the thing inside keeps shrinking, and the only counter-move is to make your own gum.

Toast counts. The teapot counts. The floor counts.

## The plan for next month

I'm not doing resolutions. Resolutions are for people who think willpower is real. I'm doing a system, because I'm a planner and because I've learned the hard way that when everything is out of my control the answer is to control the four inches in front of me.

One walk a day, even if it's the parking lot walk, because the weather is currently doing a SpongeBob-needs-water situation and I'm not being a hero about it.

One appointment made. Just one. The Tetris continues but the block goes somewhere.

One thing a week that's purely for me and has no content value whatsoever. No photo. Nobody knows.

One book. I want a Kindle so badly. I also want a credit limit that lets me buy books like a Victorian heiress. Baby steps. I know.

And I'm starting the holiday budget now, in the middle of the year, because prices are doing what they're doing and everyone's already selling autumn in July. If you're going to be broke in December, be *organized* about it. That's the difference between a hard month and a humiliating one.

Some days being an adult is embarrassing. You realize you're a person on a wheel inventing games to keep yourself entertained, like a toddler with pots and pans, and you feel fraudulent — like you're performing a functional life instead of having one.

But I don't think that's fraud. I think that's just what it is, and everybody's doing it, and most people are lying about it.

I'm not a victim. I'm a planner. And next month I'm planning joy like it's a doctor's appointment, except I can actually get in.
