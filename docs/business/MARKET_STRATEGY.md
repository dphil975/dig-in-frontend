### Prepared for: Olive Phillips | July 2026

## QUICK SUMMARY (Read This First)

Dig In has **two types of customers** — and that's what makes it powerful:

1. **Regular people** who use the app for free (or pay a small amount) to discover food, earn coins, and cook recipes
2. **Grocery stores and food brands** who pay real money every month for data about what people in their neighborhood are buying

The more regular people use the app → the more data is collected → the more valuable the reports are to grocery stores → the more money Dig In makes. This is the flywheel.

---

## PART 1: TARGET AUDIENCE

### Who Uses the App (Consumer Side)

The core consumer is someone who checks their phone before going to the store. They're not just cooking — they're looking for something. They want to know what to make, where to get it cheapest, and they want to feel rewarded for doing it.

**Primary audience: "Food-Curious Millennials & Gen Z" (Ages 18–38)**

They share these traits:
- Shop at 2+ different stores per week (comparing prices)
- Use food content on TikTok and Instagram regularly
- Have tried at least one meal kit or food delivery app
- Own a smartphone, use apps daily
- Live in urban or suburban areas near multiple grocery options
- Like games, rewards, and being part of a community
- Cook at home 3–5 nights a week but still crave variety
- Budget-conscious but not frugal — they'll splurge on one cool ingredient

**Secondary audiences:**
- Home cooks who collect global recipes and want to try new cuisines
- Budget-focused families who need ingredient substitutions
- Gen Z users drawn in by the pixel art creator (even if they care less about food)
- Health-conscious shoppers tracking what goes into their meals

**Geographic focus (launch phase):** New York City and Los Angeles — high density, lots of grocery options, food-culture rich, ethnically diverse (matches the 70+ country recipe database).

---

### Who Pays the Big Money (B2B Side)

**Primary B2B customer: Independent and regional grocery stores**

These are the stores that can't afford Kroger's loyalty program but desperately want the same customer insights. They have no way to know what the neighborhood is buying unless they look at their own receipts — which only shows THEIR customers, not the whole area.

Dig In gives them that neighborhood-wide view.

**Secondary B2B customer: CPG (Consumer Packaged Goods) brands**

These are companies like spice makers, sauce brands, specialty food producers. They want to know: "Which zip codes are buying a lot of Thai food? Where should we expand distribution?" Dig In's data answers that.

## PART 3: REVENUE PER USER (RPU) — HOW DIG IN MAKES MONEY

### What RPU Means (Simple Version)

RPU = Revenue Per User. It's how much money, on average, each person who uses the app earns for the company per month or per year. The goal is to grow this number over time.

---

### Revenue Stream 1: Consumer Subscriptions (Freemium Model)

| Tier | Price | What They Get |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Free | $0 | Map, basic recipes, 50 coins to start, pixel art basics, 3 check-ins/day |
| Premium | $4.99/month | Unlimited check-ins, full recipe database, exclusive outfits, advanced craving compass, no ads |
| Premium Annual | $39.99/year | Same as Premium, saves ~$20 vs. monthly |

**Expected conversion rate:** 12–18% of active users will go premium (industry standard for food/lifestyle apps is 10–20%).

---

### Revenue Stream 2: Coin Packs (In-App Purchases)

Users who want outfits faster than they can earn coins will buy them.

| Pack | Price | Coins |
|------|-------|-------|
| Starter Pack | $1.99 | 120 coins |
| Boost Pack | $4.99 | 350 coins |
| Big Bag | $9.99 | 800 coins |

**Expected behavior:** 8–12% of active users make at least one coin purchase per month.

---

### Revenue Stream 3: B2B Data Reports

This is where the real money is. One grocery store paying $299/month is worth more than 60 free users.

| Plan | Price/Month | Who Buys It |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Basic | $299 | 1 independent store, 1 zip code report |
| Standard | $799 | 1–5 stores, regional trends, monthly PDF report |
| Premium | $1,999 | 6–20 stores, API access, custom CPG filters |
| Enterprise | Custom (est. $5,000+) | Large chains, CPG brands with national data needs |

---

### Revenue Stream 4: Sponsored Recipes & Featured Products (Future)

CPG brands pay to have their product featured in a recipe. Example: "This pozole recipe is brought to you by Rancho Gordo beans." The user sees it as a suggestion, not an ad.

Estimated rate: **$500–$2,000 per recipe feature** depending on reach.

---

### The RPU Math

**For consumer users (blended average, per month):**

Assuming 1,000 active monthly users:
- 750 free users → $0 direct revenue
- 150 premium subscribers → $4.99 each = $748.50
- 100 coin pack buyers → avg $4.00 each = $400.00
- **Monthly consumer revenue: $1,148.50 from 1,000 users**
- **Blended consumer RPU: $1.15/user/month = $13.80/user/year**

With data value factored in (B2B revenue divided across the active users generating it), each active user is worth approximately **$18–22/year** in total monetizable value.

**For B2B clients (annualized):**

| If you have... | Monthly B2B Revenue | Annual B2B Revenue |
|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 5 Basic stores | $1,495 | $17,940 |
| 5 Basic + 3 Standard | $3,892 | $46,700 |
| 10 Standard + 2 Premium | $11,990 | $143,880 |
| 20 Standard + 5 Premium + 1 Enterprise | $25,785 | $309,420 |

**The goal:** Get to 10,000 active consumers (powering the data) + 20 B2B clients. That combination generates roughly $300K–$400K ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) — a viable Series A story.

## PART 4: HOW TO MARKET DIG IN

### The Core Marketing Problem

Nobody wakes up wanting a "dual-sided food data platform." They wake up wanting to cook dinner. Marketing has to lead with the fun consumer story — the B2B side sells itself once the data exists.

**The message that works:**
> *"Discover food. Earn coins. Explore your neighborhood."*

Not: "We collect anonymized behavioral data for retail intelligence." That's for the B2B pitch. Consumer marketing has to be fun first.

---

### Channel 1: TikTok (Priority #1 — Zero Cost to Start)

This is your fastest path to users. The pixel art creator is inherently shareable content.

**What to post:**
- Speed videos of making a pixel food character
- "Rate my pixel art food" challenges
- "I cooked a recipe from [random country] — here's how it went"
- Check-in streak showcases (#DayX)
- Side-by-side: "What I craved vs. what the app gave me"

**Format:** Short (15–30 sec), quick cuts, trending audio. No budget needed to start.

**Goal:** 3–5 videos/week for 3 months consistently. One viral video (500K+ views) brings in enough users to prove traction.

---

### Channel 2: Instagram (Companion to TikTok)

Post the same content as TikTok but leaned toward recipe aesthetic. Instagram is where the 28–38 crowd spends time. Reels drive discovery; Stories drive engagement.

**Key content format:** The "pixel art reveal" — a nice photo of finished food + the pixel version of it side by side. This is extremely shareable and on-brand.

---

### Channel 3: Micro-Influencer Partnerships (Budget: $500–$2,000/month)

Don't go for big food influencers. Target:
- Food creators with 5,000–50,000 followers (higher engagement, more trust)
- Pixel art creators and gaming creators (crossover audience)
- Budget cooking creators (Elena's audience — they need the substitution engine)
- Cultural food creators (Korean food, West African food, Latin food — matches the recipe database)

**The deal:** Give them 3 months of free Premium + $100–300 per post. They create authentic content; you get their audience.

---

### Channel 4: Grocery Store In-Store Marketing (B2B Growth Hack)

When you sign your first B2B store client, ask them for one thing: a small QR code sign near the entrance. "Earn coins when you shop here. Download Dig In." The store benefits because their shoppers generate the data — and you get free user acquisition at the exact moment someone is about to shop.

This is the loop:
1. Store pays Dig In for data → 
2. Store lets Dig In put QR code in their store → 
3. More shoppers download the app → 
4. More data for the store → 
5. Store values the data more → repeat

---

### Channel 5: App Store Optimization (ASO — Free)

Before anything else, the App Store listing needs to be optimized. This is free and often ignored.

Keywords to target: "food discovery app," "recipe finder," "grocery rewards," "pixel art game," "cooking app with rewards," "ingredient substitution."

Screenshots should lead with: the pixel creator, the map with store pins, and the coin reward screen. These are the most visually compelling.

---

### Channel 6: Referral Program (Built Into the App)

Every user who invites a friend both get 50 bonus coins. This costs nothing but coins (which are virtual) and turns every user into a marketer. The message: "I'm on Dig In earning coins at [store] — use my code and we both get 50 coins."

## PART 5: WHAT YOU NEED TO COMPLETE THE APP

These are the gaps between "impressive demo" and "ready to launch." They're organized by urgency.

---

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Fix Before Showing Anyone (Week 1–2)

**1. Real Authentication**
Right now Google Sign-In is a fake timer that returns fake data. This has to be replaced with real Supabase Auth or Firebase Auth. Without this, you have no real users — you have nobody.

*What it takes:* Connect Supabase auth (you already have a Supabase client file) to the Google OAuth provider. About 1–2 days of work.

**2. Real Social Feed**
The social feed currently generates random fake posts. If any real user sees this, they'll know immediately and uninstall. Replace with real user-generated posts — even if there are only 5 real users at launch, it has to be real.

*What it takes:* Connect the social feed to a real Supabase table that stores actual posts. About 2–3 days.

**3. Real Leaderboard**
The leaderboard has hardcoded fake users (MOCK_USERS). If Marcus (Profile 2) is #4 and discovers the competition isn't real, he's gone and he'll tell people.

*What it takes:* Query a real user_scores table from Supabase. 1 day.

**4. Deploy the Flask Backend**
All three backend API URLs in the app default to empty string — the backend isn't connected to the live app. The substitution engine, grocery locator, and data monetization system don't work in production.

*What it takes:* Deploy the Python backend to Railway.app or Render.com (both free tier available). Set the Vercel environment variables. About 1 day.

---

### 🟡 IMPORTANT — Fix Before First Real Users (Week 3–4)

**5. Payment Processing**
You need a way to take money for Premium subscriptions and coin packs. Stripe is the standard.

*What it takes:* Integrate Stripe Checkout for subscriptions + one-time coin pack purchases. Add a Supabase table to track subscription status. About 3–4 days.

**6. Privacy Policy & Terms of Service**
You cannot collect behavioral data (the entire B2B business model) without a legally compliant privacy policy. This isn't optional — it's legally required.

*What it takes:* Use a privacy policy generator (Termly or Iubenda, ~$10–30/month) customized for data collection. Have the attorney you're meeting today review it. Essential before launch.

**7. Real Data Collection Pipeline**
The DataMonetization Python module is built but it's not connected to the live app events. Every check-in, recipe view, and substitution search needs to write to the anonymized data store.

*What it takes:* Wire the frontend event triggers (check-in, recipe cook, substitution) to the backend data pipeline. About 3–5 days.

**8. Mobile Optimization**
The app needs to feel native on iPhone and Android. Right now it's built as a web app — some pages don't scroll correctly on mobile and the touch targets (buttons) are too small.

*What it takes:* CSS audit pass for mobile breakpoints, touch target sizing to 44px minimum, fix any layout breaks on small screens. About 2–3 days.

---

### 🟢 GROWTH FEATURES — Add After Launch (Month 2–3)

**9. Push Notifications**
"Your daily quest is ready!" and "Your streak is about to break!" — these are the retention hooks that bring people back. Without them, people forget the app exists.

**10. Onboarding Flow**
New users need a short (3-step) setup: "What do you like to cook?" → "What's your nearest grocery store?" → "Here's your first quest." Currently there's no real onboarding.

**11. First B2B Dashboard**
A simple web dashboard where grocery store clients can log in and see their monthly report. Currently the reports are generated by the Python module but there's no way for a client to access them.

**12. Barcode Scanner (Live)**
The scanner is built but needs a product database connection (Open Food Facts API is free) to return real ingredient data.

## PART 6: GROWTH STRATEGY

### The Simple Version

Grow in three phases:

**Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Prove It Works Somewhere**
Pick ONE city. Launch there. Get 500 real users. Sign 2 local B2B grocery clients using those 500 users' data. Prove the loop works.

**Phase 2 (Months 4–9): Grow the City, Add Cities**
Go from 500 to 5,000 users in your launch city. Use TikTok content and referral codes. Add 2 more cities using the same playbook. Sign 10 B2B clients.

**Phase 3 (Months 10–18): Scale the B2B**
Once you have data in 5 cities, the B2B pitch gets much easier. Start pursuing regional grocery chains and CPG brands. Hire a B2B sales person as your first employee.

---

### The Viral Growth Loop

This is the single most important mechanic for growing fast without spending money:

```
User shares pixel art on TikTok/Instagram
       ↓
New person sees it, downloads app
       ↓
New person earns coins by checking into stores
       ↓
New person creates pixel art and shares it
       ↓
(repeat)
```

Every new user who shares their pixel art is an ad you didn't pay for. The pixel art creator IS the marketing engine. This is why it needs to keep getting new content — new outfits, new seasonal items, limited-edition drops.

---

### The B2B Sales Strategy

You don't need a sales team at first. You need a one-page PDF and a willingness to walk into grocery stores.

**Step 1:** Find 5 independent grocery stores in your launch neighborhood. Walk in and ask for the owner or manager. 

**Step 2:** Say: "I have a data product that tells you what your neighborhood is buying — not just your customers, your whole zip code. I'm offering the first 3 months for free in exchange for a QR code at your entrance."

**Step 3:** After 3 months, show them their report. If the data is valuable, convert them to a paid plan.

**The reason this works:** Independent grocery store owners are running their business entirely on instinct and supplier conversations. Real neighborhood data is something they've literally never had access to before.
