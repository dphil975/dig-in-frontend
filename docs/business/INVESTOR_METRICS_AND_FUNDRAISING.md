## PART 1: THE METRICS THAT MATTER

Every investor — whether it's an angel putting in $10K or a VC writing a $500K check — asks the same questions. The answers live in your metrics. Here's every number you need to know, what it means, and what "good" looks like for a food app at your stage.

---

### TIER 1: THE NUMBERS INVESTORS ASK ABOUT IN THE FIRST 5 MINUTES

**MRR — Monthly Recurring Revenue**
What it is: All your subscription + B2B revenue added up, for one month. Does NOT include one-time coin pack purchases.
What good looks like:
- Pre-seed raise: $1K–$5K MRR (proves it's real)
- Seed raise: $10K–$30K MRR (proves it scales)
- Series A: $100K+ MRR

Why it matters: MRR is the single most important number. Investors use it to calculate your annual run rate (MRR × 12) and your growth rate. If your MRR grows 15–20% month over month, that's a fundable trajectory.

**MRR Growth Rate**
What it is: How much your MRR grew from last month to this month, as a percentage.
Formula: (This month's MRR − Last month's MRR) ÷ Last month's MRR × 100
What good looks like: 15–25% month-over-month at early stage. 10% is acceptable. Under 5% raises questions.

At 20% monthly growth, $1K MRR becomes $100K MRR in 26 months. That's the math investors are doing in their head when they ask this.

**MAU — Monthly Active Users**
What it is: How many unique users opened the app at least once in the last 30 days.
What good looks like: Growth. Absolute number matters less than the trend. 500 MAU growing 30% month-over-month is better than 5,000 MAU flat.

**DAU/MAU Ratio (Stickiness)**
What it is: Daily active users divided by monthly active users. Tells you if people are habitually using your app or just occasionally dipping in.
Formula: DAU ÷ MAU × 100 = stickiness %
Benchmarks:
- 20%+ = good for a lifestyle app
- 30%+ = great
- 50%+ = exceptional (WhatsApp/Instagram territory)

For a food app, 25–35% is a realistic and strong target. Check-in streaks and leaderboards naturally drive this up.

**D1 / D7 / D30 Retention**
What it is: What percentage of new users are still using the app 1 day, 7 days, and 30 days after signing up.
What good looks like:
- D1: 40%+ (industry average is ~25%)
- D7: 20%+ (industry average is ~10%)
- D30: 10%+ (industry average is ~5%)

This is the metric that separates good apps from great ones. If your D30 retention is 15%, you have something people love. If it's 3%, you have a leaky bucket — you can pour in as many new users as you want and they drain out.

Your pixel art and coin system are specifically designed to improve retention — the collection mechanic creates a reason to come back daily.

**CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost**
What it is: How much you spend on average to get one new user.
Formula: Total marketing spend ÷ New users acquired

At your stage, target CAC under $3 for free users. If you're doing organic TikTok and QR codes at grocery stores, your CAC should be near $0 (time cost only, no cash).

When you eventually run paid ads, keep CAC under $8 for a free user or under $25 for a premium user.

**LTV — Lifetime Value**
What it is: How much revenue one average user generates before they stop using the app.
Rough formula: Average monthly revenue per user × Average months retained

Example: $1.15/month blended × 18 months retained = $20.70 LTV

For the business to be healthy: LTV should be at least 3× your CAC.

**LTV:CAC Ratio**
If CAC = $5 and LTV = $20, your ratio is 4:1. That's healthy.
If CAC = $5 and LTV = $8, your ratio is 1.6:1. You're barely breaking even on each user.
Investors want to see 3:1 minimum. 5:1+ is very attractive.

**Churn Rate**
What it is: The percentage of paying subscribers who cancel in a given month.
Formula: Cancelled subscribers ÷ Total subscribers at start of month × 100
What good looks like: Under 5% monthly churn (that's 60% annual — not great but typical early stage). Under 3% is good. Under 2% is excellent.

Churn is the enemy of passive income. A 10% monthly churn means half your subscribers are gone in 7 months.

**NRR — Net Revenue Retention (B2B metric)**
What it is: Are your B2B clients spending more over time, or less?
Formula: (Start of month B2B revenue + expansions − downgrades − cancellations) ÷ Start of month B2B revenue × 100
What good looks like: 100% = you kept everything. 120%+ = clients are spending more (expanding). Under 80% = serious problem.

B2B SaaS companies with 120%+ NRR are gold to investors because it means growth happens even without new sales.

### TIER 2: OPERATIONAL METRICS (YOU TRACK THESE — INVESTORS ASK IF THEY'RE INTERESTED)

**ARPU — Average Revenue Per User**
What it is: Total monthly revenue ÷ Total active users
Your target at launch: $1.15 blended (per the financial model)

**Check-in Rate**
What it is: Average number of check-ins per active user per week. This is YOUR unique engagement metric — no other app has this.
Target: 3+ check-ins per week per active user. This means they're going to grocery stores AND using the app.

**Recipe Completion Rate**
What it is: How many users who view a recipe actually complete it (mark it as cooked). Target: 15–25%. If it's under 10%, recipes may be too complex or the completion flow has friction.

**Pixel Art Creation Rate**
What it is: Percentage of active users who have created at least one pixel art item.
Target: 40%+ of users should engage with the pixel creator within 30 days of joining. This is your virality engine.

**Coin Velocity**
What it is: Average coins earned and spent per active user per week.
Why it matters: High coin velocity means users are engaged in the economy. Low coin velocity means they signed up and stopped caring.

**B2B Report Delivery Rate**
What it is: Did every B2B client get their report on time this month? Should be 100% — this is your reputation.

**Burn Rate**
What it is: How much cash you spend per month running the business.
At your current stage: ~$50–150/month (nearly zero). This is a major asset when pitching — you're capital efficient.

**Runway**
What it is: How many months you can operate before running out of money.
Formula: Cash in bank ÷ Monthly burn rate
If you have $2,000 saved and burn $100/month, you have 20 months of runway. That's excellent for early stage.

---

### TIER 3: THE METRIC THAT WILL DEFINE YOUR STORY TO INVESTORS

**Gross Margin**
What it is: What percentage of each dollar of revenue you keep after direct costs.
For software: should be 70–90% (very high — this is why software businesses are valuable)
For Dig In: Your costs are basically infrastructure ($100–350/month). Revenue of $5,000/month means ~93% gross margin. That's exceptional.

When an investor sees 90%+ gross margin, they know every new dollar of revenue is almost all profit. That's what makes them write checks.

## PART 2: HOW TO PASSIVELY ATTRACT INVESTORS

Passive investment means investors find you and come to you — not the other way around. Here's how to build that funnel without spending money or cold-emailing VCs.

---

### Strategy 1: Build In Public on TikTok (Your Biggest Lever)

This is the most powerful passive investor magnet available to you right now, and it costs nothing.

What "building in public" means: You document the journey of building Dig In — the wins, the struggles, the milestones — as short videos. You become the face of the app.

What to post:
- "I built a pixel food app in 30 days and here's what happened" (origin story)
- "I walked into 3 grocery stores to pitch my app. Here's what they said."
- "I got my first paying customer today. It's a grocery store in Brooklyn."
- "I'm sleeping in my NYC office to build this app. Day 14."
- "Our app just hit 1,000 users. Here's every number behind that."
- Cooking a recipe from the app at a hostel (your existing idea — perfect content)
- The behind-the-scenes of building new features

Why investors watch TikTok: Angels and early-stage investors actively scout TikTok for founders with authentic stories and organic traction. Your story — young founder, pixel food app, sleeping in an office in NYC, cooking at hostels — is genuinely compelling content. You are the product as much as the app is.

When a video takes off (even 50K views), your DMs will have investors in them. This is not hypothetical — it happens regularly.

---

### Strategy 2: Wefunder or Republic (Crowdfunding = Passive Investment)

This is the most direct answer to "how do I passively get people to invest."

Wefunder (wefunder.com) and Republic (republic.com) are regulated investment crowdfunding platforms. You list Dig In, set a minimum raise (say $50K), and regular people can invest as little as $100.

Why this works for you:
- Your TikTok audience becomes investors (they love the app + they want to profit from it)
- Your grocery store B2B clients can invest (they're already using the product)
- Users who check in every day can invest $100 and feel ownership
- The campaign page itself drives app downloads (free marketing)
- You can raise $500K–$1.07M from the crowd without giving up board control

What you need to launch a campaign:
- Working app (you have this)
- Cap table (who owns what — just you right now)
- Financial projections (you have the financial model)
- A compelling video (your TikTok content repurposed)
- An LLC or C-Corp entity (discuss with your attorney today)

Timeline: 60–90 days from applying to live campaign.

Investment crowdfunding is specifically designed for founders who don't have VC connections. It turns your community into your investors.

---

### Strategy 3: AngelList (Passive Discovery by Angels)

Create a profile on AngelList Raise (raise.angellist.com). This is where angel investors actively browse for deals.

Your listing should include:
- App name, one-liner, and category (Food & Beverage / Consumer Tech)
- Key metrics (MRR, MAU, growth rate)
- Your funding ask
- Your background

Angels on AngelList move fast — if your metrics are growing and your story is compelling, they'll reach out. You don't have to chase them.

Cost: Free to list.

---

### Strategy 4: Product Hunt Launch (Passive Investor Discovery + User Acquisition)

Product Hunt (producthunt.com) is where tech people — including investors — discover new apps. A good launch gets you in front of thousands of people in 24 hours.

When to launch: Once real auth is working and the app is polished.

A top-10 Product Hunt finish means:
- Thousands of new signups (many from tech-adjacent people who talk about apps)
- Media coverage from food and tech blogs
- Investors who specifically watch Product Hunt for new consumer apps
- A permanent "Featured on Product Hunt" badge you can put in all pitch materials

---

### Strategy 5: Food Tech Press (Earned Media = Passive Investor Inbound)

When a food journalist writes about Dig In, investors read it. Here's your target press list:

- **Eater** (eater.com) — covers food culture and food apps actively
- **The Infatuation** — food discovery, huge NYC audience
- **Grub Street** (NY Mag) — NYC food culture
- **TechCrunch** — covers consumer apps, especially ones with unique angles
- **Fast Company** — covers mission-driven startups
- **Food & Wine** — covers food technology

Your pitch angle to journalists: "I built a pixel art food app that turns grocery shopping into a game — and I'm selling anonymized grocery behavior data to local stores so they can compete with Amazon Fresh." That's a story. Lead with the data angle to tech press, lead with the pixel art angle to food press.

One TechCrunch article drives more investor inbound than 6 months of cold emailing VCs.

---

### Strategy 6: YC Application (The Biggest Passive Investment Unlock)

Y Combinator is the most famous startup accelerator in the world. They give $500K in funding for ~7% equity, connect you with the best investors in Silicon Valley, and provide 3 months of intense support.

You should apply. Here's why:
- The application is free
- They accept solo founders (you don't need a co-founder)
- Consumer food apps with unique data angles are exactly what they fund
- Your B2B data intelligence layer makes this a "dual-sided marketplace" which YC loves
- Even getting an interview changes how investors perceive you

Apply at: ycombinator.com/apply
Batches: January (for Summer) and October (for Winter)

Even if you don't get in the first time, the application process forces you to crystallize your pitch, and many founders get in on their second or third try. Start now.

## PART 3: THE NYC PLAYBOOK — YOUR GRASSROOTS STRATEGY

You mentioned going to hostels and schools to cook recipes and build a presence. This is genuinely smart. Here's how to turn that into a real customer acquisition machine.

---

### Why NYC Is the Right City

NYC has more grocery stores per square mile than anywhere in the US. It has the highest density of food-curious, young, multicultural, social media-active people in the country. It is the B2B launch city because:

- Independent grocery stores are everywhere (not just Whole Foods — bodegas, ethnic markets, specialty food stores)
- Restaurant density means food is central to daily life and conversation
- Media is concentrated here (journalists, influencers, food critics)
- Investors live here (NYC is the second-largest startup ecosystem after Silicon Valley)

---

### The Hostel Strategy

Hostels in NYC are full of international travelers — exactly the audience who will love a global recipe app with food from 70+ countries. These people:

- Are food adventurous (they travel to eat)
- Share everything on social media (they're literally documenting their trip)
- Come from the countries whose recipes are in your app (they'll feel seen)
- Have nothing to do on Tuesday afternoon (they'll download the app and play with it)

How to execute this:
1. Contact 5–10 NYC hostels (HI NYC, Pod 39, Ace Hotel, The Jane) and offer to do a free "Pixel Food Night" — you cook 3 international recipes from the app, guests eat for free, everyone downloads Dig In
2. Set up a simple QR code on each table linking to the app
3. Film it all for TikTok (this content is gold — international food, pixel art, NYC)
4. Leave a stack of business cards with a QR code and "free premium week" code

Cost: Groceries (~$50–80 per event). That's your marketing budget.

---

### The School/University Strategy

NYC has Columbia, NYU, Fordham, The New School, Pratt, Baruch, Hunter, and dozens more. College students are your ideal early adopters — they're the Zara Maliks and Elena Reyeses from the customer profiles.

How to execute:
1. Contact student org boards (food clubs, international student associations, cooking clubs) and offer a free demo + cooking session
2. Pitch to professors teaching nutrition, food science, hospitality, or business — offer a free guest lecture on "building a food tech startup"
3. Set up a referral code specific to each school — track which schools drive the most users
4. Post flyers on campus with QR codes (it's free, and campus foot traffic is enormous)

The goal isn't just users. It's brand ambassadors. One popular student who posts about Dig In reaches their entire network.

---

### The Office Sleep Reality Check

Sleeping in your office to keep costs down while building a startup is a legitimate and time-honored founder move. Many successful companies were built this way. A few practical notes:

- NYC co-working spaces often have 24/7 access — WeWork, Industrious, and spaces like The Farm SoHo have had founders do this during early stage
- Some shared office spaces have showers on-site or nearby gyms with day passes (~$15/day at NYSC) which make this sustainable
- Coliving spaces are a middle ground — places like Common or Ollie offer furnished rooms for $1,200–1,600/month with utilities included and are cheaper than a solo apartment
- Hostels themselves rent monthly ($800–1,200/month for a bed) — and if you're already doing events there, you might negotiate free accommodation in exchange for running food nights

The real point: every dollar you don't spend on rent is a dollar that stays in the business or in your pocket. At $100/month infrastructure cost, you can reach break-even with 1 B2B client. Living cheap means you never have to take a bad deal from an investor because you're desperate.

## PART 4: THE INVESTOR NARRATIVE — HOW TO TELL YOUR STORY

When you eventually pitch, here is your story in three sentences:

"Dig In turns grocery shopping into a game — users earn coins for checking in at stores, discover global recipes, and create pixel art food sprites. Every check-in generates anonymized behavioral data that we sell to independent grocery stores as market intelligence, giving them the insights that Amazon and Whole Foods keep for themselves. We're building the network that local grocery stores have never had."

That's your pitch. Practice it until it sounds natural. You'll say it hundreds of times.
