# Dig In — Competitor Analysis
### For Patent Prior Art Discussion · July 2026

---

## YOUR SIMPLE EXPLANATION (Read This First)

**Why does the attorney need this list?**
Before a patent can be granted, someone has to check that nobody else already invented the same thing. This is called a "prior art search." By giving your attorney this list, you're showing them you've already thought about who does something similar — and more importantly, WHY your app is different from all of them.

**The key thing to know:** None of these apps do ALL the things Dig In does together. Each one does a piece. Your invention is the specific combination and the way the two sides (consumer + business data) work together.

---

## The Competitors & Why Dig In Is Different

---

### 1. Foursquare / Swarm
**What they do:** Users check in to real-world locations (restaurants, stores, venues) and earn points and badges. One of the earliest location-based gamification apps.

**What they DON'T do:**
- No food-specific intelligence or recipes
- No ingredient substitution
- No B2B retail market reports from consumer check-in data
- No pixel art or avatar creator
- Check-in data is used for Foursquare's own business, not sold as custom reports to individual retailers

**Why Dig In is different:** Dig In's check-in data is specifically food and grocery-focused, and the anonymized data is packaged into custom reports sold directly to individual grocery retailers. The consumer product and B2B data product are designed as one integrated system from the ground up.

---

### 2. Yummly
**What they do:** Recipe discovery and personalization app. Large recipe database with dietary filtering, personalized recommendations, and a "what can I cook?" feature.

**What they DON'T do:**
- No check-in mechanic or gamification tied to real store visits
- No B2B data monetization
- No grocery behavioral data collection
- No pixel art or avatar system
- No craving-to-store-price pipeline

**Why Dig In is different:** Yummly is a passive recipe browser. Dig In connects the recipe to the real-world store visit, the coin economy, and the data collection pipeline. The loop is: discover → visit store → check in → cook → data generated.

---

### 3. Numerator
**What they do:** Retail consumer insights company. Collects purchase behavior data (mainly through receipt scanning) and sells it to CPG brands and retailers as market intelligence.

**What they DON'T do:**
- No consumer-facing app with engagement features
- No gamification to incentivize data generation
- No recipe component
- No pixel art or avatar
- Data collection is passive (receipt upload) not tied to an engaging consumer product

**Why Dig In is different:** Numerator collects data through boring receipt scans. Dig In generates the same category of data through an engaging, gamified consumer app that people want to use. The consumer engagement is what makes the data collection sustainable and scalable.

---

### 4. Instacart
**What they do:** Grocery delivery and pickup platform. Users browse groceries, add to cart, and schedule delivery. Has a recipe feature that auto-adds ingredients to cart.

**What they DON'T do:**
- No gamification or coin rewards for store visits
- No B2B market intelligence reports from anonymized consumer behavior
- No pixel art creator
- No craving-to-store pipeline with price comparison
- No substitution engine

**Why Dig In is different:** Instacart facilitates online transactions. Dig In tracks and gamifies in-person store visits. These are fundamentally different behaviors generating fundamentally different data.

---

### 5. Whatnot
**What they do:** Gamified live-streaming shopping app. Buyers participate in live auctions for collectibles (trading cards, toys, sneakers). Has coins, rewards, and community features.

**What they DON'T do:**
- Not food-related
- No grocery or recipe component
- No B2B data intelligence product
- No map or store locator
- No pixel art food creator

**Why Dig In is different:** The only overlap is the use of a coin/gamification economy tied to real-world commercial behavior. The specific application (food discovery + grocery behavioral data) and the B2B intelligence layer are entirely distinct.

---

### 6. Kroger Loyalty App / Safeway Club Card Apps
**What they do:** Major grocery chain loyalty apps. Users scan their card at checkout to earn points, unlock discounts, and receive personalized offers. The store collects purchase data.

**What they DON'T do:**
- Single-store only (data is siloed to that chain)
- No cross-retailer data aggregation
- No recipe component or cooking guidance
- No pixel art or avatar creator
- Not available to independent or small grocery stores

**Why Dig In is different:** Dig In collects cross-retailer, cross-neighborhood data — it can tell a store how they compare to their competitors across an entire zip code. Individual loyalty apps can only see their own customers.

---

### 7. Mealime / Paprika / Plan to Eat (Meal Planning Apps)
**What they do:** Help users plan weekly meals, generate shopping lists, and organize recipes.

**What they DON'T do:**
- No real-world store check-ins or gamification
- No B2B data product
- No behavioral data collection
- No map or store locator
- No pixel art

**Why Dig In is different:** These are personal organization tools. Dig In is a data platform disguised as a fun app.

---

## Summary: The Gap in the Market

No existing product combines all of the following in a single system:
1. Gamified real-world grocery store check-ins that drive daily engagement
2. Passive, anonymized behavioral data collection during consumer interactions
3. B2B retail intelligence reports sold from that consumer-generated data
4. Recipe discovery with ingredient substitution tied to nearby store availability
5. A pixel art creator linked to a coin economy funded by the above behaviors

This specific combination — and the architecture that makes it work as a single integrated product — is where the novelty claim lives.
